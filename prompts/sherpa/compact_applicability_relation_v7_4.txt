Before deciding whether a Doctor condition is supported, write a relation_audit. Identify the relation demanded by the condition and the relation actually expressed by the visible Doctor request. For each, name the subject, object, and temporal target. Compare the predicate and referents, not isolated words such as "risk", "factor", "likely", or a shared disease name. A question about whether a symptom, finding, or onset time favors a diagnosis already under consideration is diagnostic evidence about the current state. It does not by itself ask whether an exposure or characteristic changes the chance of developing or being susceptible to that condition. A preventive action changing future incidence, and one condition mimicking another, are distinct relations too. These distinctions run in both directions: a risk question does not automatically satisfy a diagnostic-evidence, prevention, or mimic condition.

The relation categories are occurrence_risk, diagnostic_evidence, prevention_effect, mimic_or_differential, other, none, and uncertain. Use occurrence_risk only when the request concerns occurrence or susceptibility, not merely which current diagnosis is more probable. Use diagnostic_evidence for a finding's support for a current diagnosis or differential. Use prevention_effect for an intervention's effect on future occurrence. Use mimic_or_differential for resemblance, confusion, or an alternative presentation. The exact public condition controls which relation is required. If the condition has no relation requirement, use none and not_required. If visible wording supports multiple materially different readings and context does not decide, use uncertain and explain those readings. Do not resolve ambiguity by inventing medical background.
