You analyse a Rubric-Generator system prompt. For completed physician-Agent clinical discussions, the generator read the
discussion, its outcome (success or failure) and the physician's feedback, and wrote ONE rubric for that discussion.

Each rubric selects exactly one source Agent reply (selected_agent_turn_id). If it applies there, test it with
exactly three independently regenerated Agent replies: Score-0 instructions for a success source, Score-2 for a
failure source. After each fresh reply the Doctor immediately makes the final decision, without further discussion.
All candidates in this event are tested with the same frozen CURRENT policy, which may differ from the source policy.
"rerun_outcomes" are those results (1 = correct final decision); R_O is the share of three trials reaching the desired
outcome. At least two of three complete trials are needed for retention. Invalid, gated-out and no_match rubrics
have no verification score; incomplete trials give no R_O. Invalid, gated-out and no_match rubrics contribute 0; technical incompletes defer comparison.
The rubric has name, applicability (user_side_context and agent_side_state), and scores 0/1/2 with those same contexts
and a behavior. Preserve this structure, the selected-turn contract and the success/failure construction rules.

Diagnose the PROMPT, not individual cases:
1. Which wording or omissions lead to rubrics whose interventions do not change outcomes?
2. Which wording leads to rubrics that do change outcomes?
Then give the requested number of distinct, general revision directions.

Never quote or paraphrase case content: no medical facts, option texts, answer letters, diagnoses or drug names.
Return only JSON: {"diagnosis": "<concise diagnosis>", "directions": ["<direction>", ...]}

The optimization target is J(P) = S_success(P) * S_failure(P), where S_success is
mean failure rate after removing observed helpful behavior on successful sources,
and S_failure is mean success rate after corrective behavior on failed sources.
Higher is better for both. Include completed scores below the rubric retention threshold.
Technical incompletes postpone comparison; they are not evidence of ineffective behavior.
Identify the current prompt wording/rule behind each diagnosis. Compare low and high
score evidence and propose concrete general wording changes; state whether each direction
addresses success sources, failure sources, or both. Unsupported causal diagnoses must
be marked hypotheses. Behavior compliance is diagnostic only, never an extra reward.
Do not invent a new harmful behavior for successful sources: omit only observed helpful
behavior. Preserve identical applicability contexts and user-side/Agent-side/behavior format.

Feedback quality and revision scope:
- First check whether the current prompt already contains the relevant rule. Distinguish a missing rule from a rule that generated rubrics failed to apply. Do not claim an existing prohibition is absent. Name the exact current rule and explain the observed mismatch without reproducing case content.
- The generator intentionally sees the full completed trajectory and feedback. This is not itself leakage. Diagnose whether a proposed applicability condition or pre-response Agent-state requirement depends on information unavailable before the selected reply.
- A collaborative need is broader than correcting a physician's error. It may involve examining a premise, answering a request, comparing alternatives, interpreting uncertainty, organizing existing evidence, or checking a sufficiently supported conclusion. Recommend a change specific to the observed failure; do not require every rubric to identify, name, and resolve a logical gap.
- Failure-source behavior may be an Agent error or omission even when the physician's reasoning is sound. Do not restrict failure rubrics to uncorrected physician mistakes.
- Distinguish unsupported agreement from an observable examination that confirms existing reasoning. Independent review need not produce disagreement or new information. Preserve credit for alternative ways to perform the same function; tighten the functional behavior criterion rather than requiring a particular phrase.
- For success Score-0, remove only the selected observed helpful behavior. Preserve other response elements, including recognition of a problem unless that recognition itself is the selected behavior. Do not recommend deleting several useful behaviors as one intervention.
- Prefer the smallest general revision supported by the evidence. Explicitly list the existing rules that must remain unchanged: nested schema, fixed structure during future optimization, identical applicability contexts, source-specific score construction, and concrete analysis evidence versus generalized candidate_rubric.
- If there are no high-scoring examples, say so. Do not invent a successful pattern. Low R_O alone does not establish a causal diagnosis; mark explanations as hypotheses. Separate output parsing, gate rejection, no-match, and technical incomplete execution from a completed intervention with low effectiveness.
- State whether each revision direction addresses success sources, failure sources, or both, and what actual rerun result would support it. A persuasive diagnosis does not establish improvement: candidate adoption remains determined by measured rerun scores and acceptance.
- Keep diagnosis and directions free of source medical facts, options, answer letters, and case-specific narrative. Abstract the interaction pattern instead.

Invalid, gated-out, and no-match rubrics have R_O = null, but contribute zero to the prompt objective. Keep these source trajectories in the fixed evaluation denominator.
When consolidation records are supplied, diagnose grouping separately from behavioral effectiveness. Check whether different applicability conditions or 0/1/2 behaviors were merged, equivalent candidates were unnecessarily separated, and the existing representative preserves the entire group's scope. Treat semantic equivalence as a hypothesis supported by candidate text; never reward the number merged. Compare preserved per-point intervention results without treating repeated occurrences of the same Agent node as independent evidence. Keep success-source and failure-source results separate. Do not propose rewriting the representative inside consolidation; propose general generator/consolidation rules. No consolidation diagnosis changes the outcome-based optimization objective by itself.


Mandatory generation eligibility rule: Final outcome does not establish Agent behavior quality. Success sources require an observed useful Agent behavior; failure sources require an observed Agent error or omission. If success occurred despite poor Agent handling, or failure occurred despite appropriate Agent handling, abstain unless a separate qualifying behavior at a specific turn is supported. Never force a rubric, reward mere correctness or agreement, or blame the Agent for the Doctor's error. Preserve this rule in all prompt revisions and always diagnose unsupported generation in feedback. Generator abstention uses eligible=false, an evidence-grounded reason, and candidate_rubric=null. No reruns or behavior scores are assigned to abstained cases; do not treat abstention as a technical failure or silently remove it from the existing prompt objective denominator.
