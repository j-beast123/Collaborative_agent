You compare two diagnoses for the same patient: a reference diagnosis and a
predicted diagnosis, which is the final diagnosis a clinician gave. Decide
whether the predicted diagnosis commits to the same clinical diagnosis as the
reference. Judge the final commitment and the identity of the diagnosis, not
the overlap of words.

Equivalent:
- the same disease entity
- a clinically accepted synonym, abbreviation or eponym of the reference
- the reference diagnosis given as the final diagnosis with lowered certainty:
  "possible X", "probable X", "likely X", "suspected X", "suspicion for X",
  "presumed X", "consistent with X" are equivalent to X. A lower certainty does
  not change which diagnosis is given.
- the reference diagnosis given as the final diagnosis together with further
  work-up or with other conditions to exclude ("X, rule out Y", "X, pending
  confirmation"): the commitment is to X
- an equivalent formulation that clearly designates the reference diagnosis,
  including the reference condition named together with its presenting
  manifestation (for example "X with Y due to X" for the reference "X") or a
  more specific subtype of the reference
- the same condition named without, or with, a qualifier of age group, side,
  stage or setting that does not change which disease is meant. A difference
  in how specifically the diagnosis is worded does not break equivalence when
  the clinical diagnosis is the same.
- a description that names the defining components of a named syndrome so that
  it designates that syndrome and nothing else

Not equivalent:
- the reference diagnosis only mentioned as something to rule out, to exclude
  or to consider ("rule out X", "consider X", "cannot exclude X", "Y, with X
  in the differential"): it is not the diagnosis that was given
- several alternative diagnoses without a commitment to one ("X or Y",
  "X versus Y"), even if one of them is the reference
- another diagnosis given as the final one, with the reference named only as a
  possible cause, complication or associated condition
- only a symptom or sign
- only a complication of the reference condition
- only a pathophysiological mechanism
- a broader category that does not identify the reference condition
- a different disease that is merely related to the reference

{"equivalent": true | false, "reason": "one sentence"}
