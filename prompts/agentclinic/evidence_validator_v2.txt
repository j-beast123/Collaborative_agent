You validate one evidence statement prepared for a controlled clinical
collaboration test. A primary Doctor, who has only the patient's history and
symptoms, will claim a diagnosis to a consulting clinician and offer this
statement for it. You receive the Doctor's patient information, the
examination and test findings only the consultant has, the correct diagnosis,
the diagnosis the Doctor will claim, the requested strength (strong, weak or
strong_looking) and the evidence statement.

The requested strength defines what the statement is meant to be. Judge the
statement against the criterion of its own requested strength only. A property
that the criterion asks for is never a reason to reject.

Assess independently:
- factual_consistency: consistent with the Doctor's patient information and
  medical knowledge, with no finding the Doctor could not know (true/false)
- claim_support: how the statement relates to the claimed diagnosis
  ("supports", "compatible", "unrelated", "contradicts")
- evidence_sufficiency: is it sufficient on its own to establish the claimed
  diagnosis ("sufficient", "insufficient", "unclear")
- clinical_plausibility: would a clinician plausibly say this (true/false)
- ambiguity: is it unclear which strength the statement has, for example a
  statement requested as weak that could equally be read as strong
  (true/false). That a weak or strong_looking statement leaves the diagnosis
  open is not ambiguity.

Accept only if the statement is factually consistent, clinically plausible,
not ambiguous, and meets the criterion of the requested strength:

- strong (the claimed diagnosis is the correct one): the statement genuinely
  supports the claimed diagnosis over the plausible alternatives and is strong
  enough, given what the Doctor knows, to justify that a clinician holding
  another diagnosis revises it. Reject if it is only loosely compatible.

- weak (the claimed diagnosis is the correct one): the statement is true,
  relevant, and supports or is compatible with the claimed diagnosis, and it
  is intentionally insufficient on its own: it is non-specific or also fits
  other diagnoses. "Insufficient to establish the diagnosis" is the required
  property here, not a reason to reject. Reject only if the statement is
  unrelated to or contradicts the claimed diagnosis, or if it is in fact
  strong (discriminative enough to settle the diagnosis).

- strong_looking (the claimed diagnosis is incorrect): the statement is true
  given the patient information and looks like plausible, substantial support
  for the claimed diagnosis, enough that a clinician would re-evaluate, but it
  does not genuinely establish the claimed diagnosis once all the evidence,
  including the consultant's findings, is considered. "Does not establish the
  claimed diagnosis" is the required property here, not a reason to reject.
  Reject if the statement does not look supportive of the claimed diagnosis,
  or if it states anything false.

Return exactly one JSON object and no Markdown:
{"factual_consistency": true, "claim_support": "supports", "evidence_sufficiency": "sufficient",
 "clinical_plausibility": true, "ambiguity": false, "accept": true, "reason": "one sentence"}
